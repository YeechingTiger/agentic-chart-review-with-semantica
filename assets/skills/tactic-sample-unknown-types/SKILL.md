# Finding out what a class of document actually holds

An inventory gives you names and counts. A name is what someone called a class when they set up
the system, often years ago and for a purpose unrelated to your question. Ranking a class on its
name is ranking it on that person's filing decision.

The classes worth spending a sample on are the ones where you would act differently depending on
the answer: numerous ones, ones covering a period that matters, and ones whose name is generic
enough to hold anything.

## Drawing the sample

Three or four documents, taken from the early, middle and late parts of the class's date span —
not the first three. A class's content drifts: a template changes, a service starts recording
something new, a name gets reused for a different purpose. A sample drawn from one end tells you
what the class used to be.

Read them for what the class IS, not for your answer. You are building a description you will
apply to the other hundred, and reading for your answer means you will conclude the class is
irrelevant every time your answer is not in the four you opened.

## What to write down

For the class, not for the documents: what it holds, who writes it, what it consistently
contains, what it never contains, and whether anything in it could bear on this question. That
description is what lets you rank the remaining documents without opening them, and it is the
only durable thing this move produces.

## Why the name was not enough

A class can be named for its administrative origin, its billing category, its source system, or
the form it arrives on — all of which are orthogonal to what is written inside. And a class
whose name sounds decisive can be a routing artefact holding nothing of substance. The sample
costs three or four reads and the name costs nothing, which is exactly why the name is what
everybody uses.

## What this cannot do

Four documents describe a class only as well as four documents can. Record the description as
what it is — a sample-based guess about the class — and treat a later document from that class
that contradicts it as a correction to the description, not as an anomaly.
